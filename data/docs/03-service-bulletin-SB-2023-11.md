# Service Bulletin SB-2023-11

**Subject:** CO₂ handling, storage and leak diagnosis
**Issued:** 2023-09-22
**Action:** Advisory

---

## Summary

This bulletin consolidates guidance on carbon dioxide handling for engineers
working on FS-500-SPK units. It does not change any published interval.

## Storage and transport

Store cylinders upright and secured, below 45 °C, out of direct sunlight. A
gas bottle stored on its side may deliver liquid CO₂ into the regulator on
opening, which will frost the line and can damage the carbonator.

Vehicles carrying more than two cylinders require ventilation to the load area.

## Diagnosing a loss of carbonation

A complaint of flat sparkling water has three common causes, in order of
frequency:

1. **Depleted cylinder.** Check the regulator gauge. Below 3.0 bar at room
   temperature the cylinder is effectively spent and requires a cylinder
   exchange.
2. **Regulator leak.** Soap-test the regulator to gas bottle joint. A slow leak
   will empty a cylinder over several days without any audible hiss.
3. **Carbonator fault.** If the gauge holds pressure and the unit still
   dispenses flat water, the carbonator pump is the likely cause.

Do not condemn a carbonator before eliminating the first two causes.

## Pressure reference

| Condition | Gauge reading at 20 °C |
| --- | --- |
| Freshly exchanged cylinder | 8.0 to 9.5 bar |
| Serviceable | 3.0 to 8.0 bar |
| Requires exchange | Below 3.0 bar |

Note that the gauge reads cylinder pressure, not remaining volume. A gas bottle
will hold close to its working pressure until it is nearly empty, then fall
quickly. Engineers should not infer remaining service life from a mid-range
reading.

## Disposal

Return every empty gas bottle to the depot. Cylinders are refilled and reused;
none are scrapped at the customer site.
